DossierSaskatchewan's sourcing rules The rules, in black and white.

The site only publishes what the Legislative Assembly, the government or a party has itself published, as is, with a link to the original. And it says clearly what is missing.

  1. 1No invented data

    This is rule number one. If a piece of information is missing, the field stays empty or the site says so. We do not guess and we do not fill in.

  2. 2Primary sources only

    We quote the institution or the party itself, never someone talking about it: the Legislative Assembly of Saskatchewan (its official minutes, the Votes and Proceedings; its bill PDFs and their explanatory notes; its MLAs page) for bills, votes and MLAs; the Government of Saskatchewan (the Cabinet page on saskatchewan.ca) for ministers; the Saskatchewan Party's official platform, published on its website, for promises; the Saskatchewan Lobbyist Registry (Office of the Registrar of Lobbyists), read with the Office's written agreement, for lobbying registrations.

  3. 3No news media

    Nothing is taken from the news media. A promise made at a press conference but never published by the party therefore does not appear on the site.

  4. 4We never work around a protection

    The program that reads the official websites identifies itself for what it is, with the address of this site. If a website answers with a CAPTCHA or an anti-bot challenge, it stops instead of insisting: nothing is written, and the previous day's data stays in place.

  5. 5Promises are quoted word for word

    Every promise is an exact quote from the party's platform. A script checks that each quote appears exactly as written in the source; a quote that cannot be found there is not published. Every card carries a “See the source” link to the original.

  6. 6No verdict

    The site never says “promise kept” or “promise broken”. It places the promise next to the bills that carry it out, each with its source. You are the judge.

  7. 7The official text always prevails

    Plain-language summaries are written by artificial intelligence from each bill's official text, and the full text is always one click away. A summary describes the bill as introduced and is not an official document: the Assembly's text is the one that counts. The site is independent and unofficial.

  8. 8We explain what is missing

    When a source is absent or incomplete, the page says so and says why. The promises page follows only the governing party, and only the 12 commitments it highlighted: it says so. A bill amended in committee carries a notice, because its summary describes the text as introduced. An absence must never be read as silence from a party.

These rules apply everywhere on the site. See them at work on the Promises page, and follow what changes in the site updates.
