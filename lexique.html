Inside the Assembly
Speaker of the Legislative Assembly

Todd Goudy

Presides over sittings and enforces the Rules. MLA for Melfort (Saskatchewan Party), but the chair requires neutrality: the Speaker only votes to break a tie.

Iris Lang

The Assembly's top non-partisan official since January 1, 2023: advises the Speaker and members on procedure and keeps the official record, including the minutes this site reads every day.

The lobbyist registry

Who's paid to influence public decisions — and how to look it up

Anyone paid to lobby Saskatchewan public office holders (ministers, MLAs, senior officials…) must record their activities in an online public registry, kept by the Registrar of Lobbyists (Maurice Herauf, see above), an independent officer of the Assembly. Volunteers and citizens pursuing their own interests don't have to register.

Why this site doesn't reproduce the registry: it can only be consulted through the official search tool. Rather than make a copy that wouldn't be reliable or up to date, we link straight to the official public tool.

Parliamentary glossary

To understand the rest of the site without a political science degree
Bill
Process
The text a member or the government submits to the Assembly to become a law. It only becomes a real law once passed and then assented to.
Passage in principle
Process
The first big stage: the Assembly votes on whether it agrees with the general idea of the bill, before studying it in clause-by-clause detail.
Clause-by-clause review
Process
The stage where a committee (a small group of MLAs) goes through the bill in detail, clause by clause, and can propose amendments.
Passage
Process
The final vote in the Chamber on the entire bill, once all previous stages are complete.
Assent
Process
The very last administrative step: the Lieutenant Governor gives official assent, and the bill formally becomes a law of Saskatchewan.
Parliamentary committee
Process
A smaller group of MLAs who study a topic or bill in depth, hear experts and citizens, before it comes back before the full Assembly.
Recorded vote
Votes
A vote where each MLA's name and choice (Yea/Nay) are officially recorded — as opposed to a voice vote, where only the overall result is noted.
Minutes (Procès-verbal)
Votes
The official but summarized record of everything that happened during a sitting: decisions, votes, tabled documents.
Debates journal (Journal des débats)
Votes
The complete, word-for-word transcript of everything said in the Chamber — unlike the minutes, which only summarize decisions.
House leader
People & roles
The MLA responsible for organizing their group's proceedings at the Assembly — a bit like the conductor of the calendar and debates for their party.
Whip
People & roles
The MLA in charge of making sure members of their caucus are present for votes and follow the party line.
Minister Delegate / Minister Responsible for
People & roles
A minister who isn't the main head of a department, but who officially holds responsibility for a specific file within or alongside it.
Attendance (%)
Citizen tools
The figure shown under each minister (e.g. “attendance: 88% (647/735)”): out of the 735 recorded votes across the legislature's 3 sessions, 647 are ones where this person appears as Yea, Nay, or Abstention — meaning they were present to vote, regardless of which way. The count starts from their very first detected vote (a way to approximate the start of their current term without external data). The Assembly does not publish official attendance; this is a presence proxy, not an official measure — the same method used by some media outlets (e.g. Radio-Canada).
